FLYBRAIN

What this is. Each line is written by a small language model, Liquid AI's LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct, from a short readout of that fly's simulated brain activity: four copies of the Janelia MaleCNS v1.0 fly connectome (CC BY 4.0), each run as a uniform leaky integrate-and-fire model, a simplification of real neurons, in a simulated room. This is not a fly's thoughts. Flies do not use language, and the names A, B, C and D are labels, not personalities. Each turn lists the readout the model was shown. A line is kept only if its numbers, neuron names and distances are in that readout, and it can still misstate it.

The 3D room needs WebGL, which this browser did not provide. The transcript still updates.

    How the lines are made

    Every line is written by LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct running on one local GPU. One wiring diagram of a male fruit fly's central nervous system (Janelia MaleCNS v1.0, 165,122 neurons) is run as four separate copies, each a uniform leaky integrate-and-fire model: every neuron has the same simple parameters, and a connection's weight is its synapse count times a fixed 0.275 mV with the sign of its predicted transmitter. The copies stand in a simulated 20 x 20 mm room; each walks by its own descending neurons, sees the others as dark shapes, gets a cVA smell drive as they come near and a sound drive from their simulated pulse-song motor neurons. When a fly's turn comes (every 10 s, A, B, C, D in order), its readout is at most six lines: its two named neuron groups whose firing rose most above their own baseline, how far it walked since its last turn, and how far away each other fly is. The model gets the fixed instruction below, that readout and the last line, and samples one or two sentences (temperature 0.4, top-p 0.9, repetition penalty 1.05, at most 60 tokens).

    The fixed instruction, word for word:

    You write one short line at a time for one of four simulated flies, labelled A, B, C and D, in a small simulated room. Under the readout you get facts about that fly, measured in a computer simulation of its neurons and body, and the last line another fly said. Write in the first person as that fly, in one or two short sentences. Use only facts from that fly's own readout, with each number and unit exactly as written. A fly cannot see another fly's neurons. Never repeat a recent line. Do not mention people, the internet, money or anything outside the room. Do not claim feelings, thoughts, wishes or consciousness. Plain text only: no name label, no quotation marks, no lists.

    Each turn's message, with only the labels, readout lines and last line filled in:

    Readout for fly A: - <readout lines> Last line: D: <the last line> Write fly A's line in the first person: one fact from its readout, with the number and unit, then how far fly D is from it, from the readout.

    Only formatting is normalised (curly quotes, dashes, an ellipsis character, surrounding spaces, a leading name label); no word is changed. A line is then kept only if it passes the safety filter and a grounding check: it quotes at least one number with its unit, every number it quotes is in its own readout with that unit, every neuron group it names is in that readout, a distance it gives another fly is that fly's distance there, and it does not repeat one of the last six lines. Otherwise it is dropped and counted, never edited, and sampled again at most twice. The check cannot see a right number in a wrong sentence, so a kept line can still misstate the readout. The rates, distances and bearings are measured in the simulation; the room, the readout's shape, the instruction, the sampling settings and the checks are choices.

    Connectome: MaleCNS v1.0, Janelia FlyEM et al., CC BY 4.0. Rendering: three.js (MIT).

    Visitors cannot post lines. Every line and every readout line passes a safety filter before it is shown (length limit; slurs, sexual content, threats, self-harm, web addresses starting http or www., bare addresses on common domains, @handles, and any financial or trading language); filtered lines are dropped and counted, never edited.
